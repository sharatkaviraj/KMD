# Payment recovery compatibility acceptance

Scope: synthetic payments only, owner/session-scoped browser recovery, no database or messaging calls. Parent requested explicit compatibility acceptance after the cross-tab recovery review.

The desk checks browser locking before enabling collection. Unsupported or denied locking, and unavailable recovery storage, pause saving with a persistent explanation and a Retry recovery action. Storage failures after review keep the entered form. No posting action runs until the request is durably stored under the cross-tab lock. Financial writes require a confirmed localStorage mirror even when IndexedDB works; a failed IndexedDB read with no mirror cannot be treated as an empty recovery record. Existing unresolved requests retain their financial fields and request ID; retry cannot replace them with a fresh request. Ordinary non-financial draft behavior is unchanged.

The browser suite covers 390px unsupported and denied locking, missing and denied storage, private-mode-like quota failure, successful recovery after restoring support, collector/session/missing-identity changes, and a temporarily denied IndexedDB after an unresolved payment (with and without its mirror). Component coverage additionally changes collector identity without remounting, blocks delayed old-scope writes and save responses while new-scope recovery is pending, and preserves existing receipt viewing on unsupported browsers. The existing lost-response/student-switch/reload, two-tab conflict, genuine review/back, four-width receipt/next-collection and permission cases remain required.

Compatibility tradeoff: an older or restricted browser without Web Locks cannot collect payments. There is no unprotected fallback. Web Locks require a secure context and have broad browser support since March 2022 ([MDN navigator.locks](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Navigator/locks)). A clerk with an unfinished payment must restore access in the original browser; entering the payment again in another browser would lose this recovery scope. Synthetic browser fault injection represents private-mode-like restrictions; it does not certify every phone vendor's private browsing implementation.

Latest-head CI and browser results must be checked before integration. A coordinated clean integration phone retest remains the release gate. No migration, LIVE operation, real payment, or WhatsApp send is part of this evidence.
